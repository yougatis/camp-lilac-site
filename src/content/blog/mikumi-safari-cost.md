Mikumi is the cheapest big-game safari in Tanzania that you can reach without flying, and it is also the one where prices are the hardest to compare, because every operator bundles a different mix of fees, transport, meals and nights. This article takes it apart line by line so you can see where the money goes, and ends with four worked budgets. Prices are correct for the 2026 season; park fees usually change on 1 July, and we update this page when they do.

## The short answer

For two or more people travelling together, in 2026 you can expect to pay roughly:

- **A day in the park from Morogoro or the camp:** from US$214 per person, everything in the park included.
- **A weekend from Dar es Salaam by train (2 days, 1 night):** about US$440 per person all-in, of which US$394 is the package.
- **The same weekend from Zanzibar with the ferry:** about US$520–600 per person; add US$320–400 if you fly both ways.
- **Three days, two nights from Dar es Salaam:** about US$520 per person all-in.

One person travelling alone pays more for the same trip, because the vehicle and guide are private and the cost is not shared. Children under 4 travel free and 4–11 pay half at Camp Lilac.

## 1. Park fees (TANAPA, 2026)

Entry to Mikumi National Park is charged per person per 24 hours, and Tanzania adds 18% VAT to every fee. Mikumi is in TANAPA’s lowest price band — the same as Ruaha, and well under half of what Nyerere (Selous) charges.

| Who | Fee per 24 hours |
|---|---|
| Non-resident adult (16+) | US$30 + 18% VAT = **US$35.40** |
| Non-resident child (5–15) | US$10 + 18% VAT = **US$11.80** |
| Child under 5 | Free |
| East African citizens and residents | Lower rates, paid in Tanzanian shillings |
| Vehicle | A separate daily fee per vehicle, plus VAT |

Two things catch people out. First, the 24-hour rule: a 2-day safari that enters the park on Saturday afternoon and leaves on Sunday afternoon is one 24-hour period, but push the Sunday drive later and it becomes two. Our guides time drives to keep it to one where possible. Second, if you sleep *inside* the park you pay entry fees for every 24 hours you are there, whether or not you go out, plus a concession fee per person per night — that is the main reason in-park lodges cost what they do. Camp Lilac is outside the park, so you pay fees only for game-drive days.

Fees are paid at the gate by card or mobile money, not cash. All our safari packages include them, so you never deal with the gate yourself.

## 3. Where you sleep

Mikumi accommodation falls into three groups, and the price gap between them is wide:

- **Inside the park:** the most expensive option. Nightly rates run to several hundred dollars per person, and TANAPA entry and concession fees apply to every night. You are in the middle of the wildlife, and some lodges offer night drives.
- **Mikumi town and the highway:** the cheapest option — basic guesthouses from a few tens of dollars, mid-range lodges with pools for more — with the trucks on the Dar–Mbeya highway as the soundtrack.
- **Bush camps outside the park:** the middle ground. At Camp Lilac a luxury tent with a private bathroom and air conditioning is US$140–200 per night per room, with breakfast and dinner included when you book direct; lunch is US$15 per person. No park or concession fees for the nights you are not in the park.

The full comparison, with the trade-offs of each group, is in [where to stay in Mikumi](/where-to-stay-in-mikumi).

## 4. The safari itself

If you arrive with your own car, Mikumi is one of the few parks you can self-drive: the floodplain circuit is well signed and passable all year. Most visitors, though, want a guide and an open-roof vehicle, and that is where quotes diverge, because a guided day in the park is priced per vehicle, per person, with or without lunch, with or without the fees.

## 6. How to spend less without a worse safari

- **Travel as a pair or a group.** The vehicle and guide are private, so the per-person price drops sharply from one person to two and again to three or four.
- **Take the train, not a car.** The train is cheaper, faster and less tiring than a private transfer from Dar es Salaam.
- **Stay three days, not two.** The third day adds the most wildlife for the least money.
- **Come in the green season.** November to February has quieter roads, dramatic skies and the same park fees; rates outside the park are lower.
- **Sleep outside the park.** You skip the concession fee and the entry fee on non-driving days, and you can arrive late by train.
